Known boundary: process kill or forced OS termination cannot await network cleanup. No recovery path is allowed to quit/power-off the Home console. Existing no-DELETE policy is retained. Cancellation of a synchronous native SDK call cannot safely free its buffers while that call is executing; native hangs remain a release blocker until device testing establishes behavior.

## Queue residence qualifications

The full stream queue/clock inventory remains in `investigation/FULL-AUDIT.md`. The relevant current capacities are: RTC commands 16, important events 32, audio batches 16; RTP pending audio 32 packets; reorder hold 24 packets; encoded AU queue 32 items /4 MiB with a 2 MiB per-AU cap; decoder commands 1; audio worker 32 Opus packets /8 PCM buffers; SDL audio existing 240 ms safety bound; voice 3 clips with 80 ms admission-age cutoff; catalog foreground/prefetch/results 4/4/8; trace/history/incidents 4096/2100/1024. Latest input and latest decoded-frame handoffs replace prior unpublished work. Output pictures use exclusive surface leases. Runtime title icons retain the existing radius-16 selection cache; disk cache count/bytes are separately bounded.

These capacities do **not** imply a maximum wall-clock residence during a stalled consumer/native call. Important RTC events may block on a full UI receiver until it drains or shutdown drops it. AU overload retains dependency-aware recovery/cutover rather than arbitrary frame eviction; the 50 ms pressure age is an observation, not a hard latency bound. Audio overload clears/drops according to existing epoch/age policies; a byte count cannot establish actual DAC freshness. Reorder waits apply to sequence holes, not every packet. Catalog admission uses nonblocking bounded sends; shutdown selects cancellation ahead of work/results. Synchronous filesystem writes and image decoding are bounded in bytes but not preemptible, so a stuck driver cannot be given a proven duration. Native/socket/sender queues outside Rust remain unmeasured. Do not sum nominal queue sizes into a claimed end-to-end latency guarantee.
